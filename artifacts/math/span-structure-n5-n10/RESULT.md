# relu-depth-frontier-research-max11-root-gmp.5 — span structure n=5..10

## Scope and outcome

All arithmetic used for the kernel, witness, collapse, and Burnside decisions is exact integer or
rational arithmetic.  The only primes used were `1000003` and `1000033`, for corroborating ranks
and for a lossless prefilter before exact integer pair-sum comparison.  Neither modular computation
is used to assert an identity over `Q`.

1. **Exact kernels / local moves: REFUTED for the precisely tested generator family.**  Complete
   exact-Q right-kernel bases were computed for n=7 and n=8.  Common-edge cancellations, exact
   one-edge/two-edge equal-column moves, and their named graph-local quadratic squares do not span
   either full kernel.  Explicit integer kernel vectors outside the tested spans are retained.
2. **Vertex-collapse lemma: PROVED.**  The unnormalized symmetrized column identity follows by
   partitioning the n! relabelings by the vertex sent to the minimum coordinate.  It passed on all
   831 / 831 complete loopless templates at n=5,6,7,8.
3. **Burnside counts: PROVED.**  The requested loopless simple-pair counts are 490,480 orbits for
   (n,k)=(12,5) and 18,436,223 orbits for (n,k)=(11,6), under the complete `S_n x C_2` quotient.

### Precisely tested local families

- A **common-edge cancellation** key deletes `A intersect B` and canonically labels the remaining
  signed pair.  The identity `max(L_A+L_C,L_B+L_C)=L_C+max(L_A,L_B)`, followed by `S_n`
  symmetrization, proves every admitted difference exactly.
- A **one-edge replacement** pair shares an orbit-invariant canonical template after deleting one
  edge from the changed branch while holding the other branch fixed.
- A **degree-preserving two-edge rewire** pair shares the canonical two-edge-deleted template and
  the deleted vertex-degree vector.
- A named **quadratic move square** is an exact relation `c_i+c_j-c_k-c_l=0` whose two opposite
  template edits are both one-edge replacements or both degree-preserving rewires.  Every exact
  pair-sum collision among distinct complete column classes was also enumerated as a stronger,
  graph-locality-free comparison.

Exact equality was checked on every matrix row before any candidate was admitted as a relation.
Naive graph edits are not automatically relations: at n=7, 8,382 / 8,591 proposed one-edge pairs
and 413 / 428 proposed rewire pairs were rejected; at n=8, 11,111 / 11,352 and 484 / 500 were
rejected.  These rejected pairs are hostile controls, not discarded trials.

## Question 2 — vertex-collapse lemma

For a loopless template `(A,B)` on vertices `{0,...,n-1}`, define
`collapse_v(A)` by deleting vertex `v`, relabeling the other vertices, retaining every nonincident
edge, and replacing each incident edge `(v,u)` by the diagonal loop `(u,u)`.  Let

`F_n(A,B)(x) = sum_{sigma in S_n} max(L_{sigma A}(x), L_{sigma B}(x))`.

Then on the sorted cone `x_0 <= ... <= x_(n-1)`, with each smaller column embedded by a leading zero
coordinate,

`F_n(A,B)(x) = sum_v F_(n-1)(collapse_v(A), collapse_v(B))(x_1,...,x_(n-1))`.

**Proof.** Partition the n! permutations in the left orbit sum by the unique original vertex `v`
sent to sorted position 0.  Each part has exactly `(n-1)!` permutations.  Because `x_0` is minimum,
an incident edge contributes `max(x_0,x_j)=x_j`, exactly the contribution of the diagonal loop at
its other endpoint.  Nonincident edges are unchanged.  Thus the part indexed by `v` is precisely
the collapsed `(n-1)`-vertex orbit sum; summing the n parts proves the identity.  This proof uses
unnormalized orbit sums; normalized averages would carry the corresponding factor `1/n`.

Machine checks covered 19 / 19 templates at n=5, 25 / 25 at n=6, 357 / 357 at n=7, and 430 / 430
at n=8, for 831 / 831 identities.  Loop-aware subset DP agreed with direct permutation evaluation
on 12 / 12 registered cases.  The hostile mutation dropping the `v=0` summand failed on the first
registered n=5 case (linear coordinate 1 was 12 instead of 24).

The direction matters: this proves that the n-vertex loopless column span maps into a collapsed
lower-arity loop-inclusive span.  The fact that the lower-arity span contains its MAX target does
not prove that the target lies in the image of the collapse map.

## Question 3 — Burnside counts

For a permutation `g`, let `f_k(g)` be the number of k-edge simple loopless graphs fixed by `g`.
Burnside over vertex relabeling and branch swap gives

`orbits = [sum_g (f_k(g)^2 + f_k(g^2))] / (2 n!)`.

The implementation sums by cycle type using exact integers.

| case | Burnside numerator / group-order denominator | exact quotient |
|---|---:|---:|
| n=12, k=5 | 469,881,409,536,000 / 958,003,200 | 490,480 orbits |
| n=11, k=6 | 1,471,830,052,492,800 / 79,833,600 | 18,436,223 orbits |

Independent direct `pynauty` orbit enumeration reproduced 19 orbits from 2,025 / 2,025 labeled
ordered graph pairs at n=5,k=2 and 25 orbits from 11,025 / 11,025 pairs at n=6,k=2.  Omitting the
branch-swap coset disagreed with the direct answer in 2 / 2 controls and was rejected.

## No-claim boundary

**No claim:** nothing in this bead decides MAX11, proves or refutes membership in the n=11
loopless k=5 family, proves that all kernel relations have any untested local form, or supplies an
all-n construction.  The n=7/n=8 local-generator failure is a bounded null for the precise
two-term/quadratic families above.  The vertex-collapse theorem is an inclusion/factorization
identity whose converse image-membership step was not proved.  The two Burnside values are orbit
counts, not rank or representability results.  No exact MAX11 identity was found or asserted.
